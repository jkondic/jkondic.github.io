Research

Granite Vision: A Lightweight, Open-Source Multimodal Model for Enterprise Intelligence
Granite Vision Team (incl. Jovana Kondic)
arXiv, 2025
arXiv

A lightweight, open-source multimodal model built around a 2B-parameter language model, designed for visual document understanding in enterprise settings. Granite Vision achieves strong performance on document extraction from tables, charts, diagrams, and infographics while remaining computationally efficient. Released under an Apache-2 license.

ChartGen: Scaling Chart Understanding Via Code-Guided Synthetic Chart Generation
Jovana Kondic, P. Li, D. Joshi, Z. He, S. Abedin, J. Sun, B. Wiesel, E. Schwartz, A. Nassar, B. Wu, A. Arbelle, A. Oliva, D. Gutfreund, L. Karlinsky, R. Feris
ICCV Workshop on Graphic Design Understanding and Generation / Workshop on Curated Data for Efficient Learning, 2025
arXiv

We present a fully automated pipeline that uses a VLM to reconstruct seed chart images into executable plotting code, then iteratively augments scripts with a code-oriented LLM to generate diverse synthetic chart-image-code pairs at scale. From just 13K seed images, ChartGen produces 222.5K unique pairs across diverse chart types and plotting libraries. We release the pipeline, prompts, and dataset to advance chart understanding research.

Lyfe Agents: Generative Agents for Low-Cost Real-Time Social Interactions
K. I. Zhao, M. Naim, Jovana Kondic, M. Ernesto Cortes, J. Ge, S. Luo, G. R. Yang, A. Ahn
arXiv, 2024
arXiv

We introduce Lyfe Agents, LLM-powered autonomous agents for real-time social interaction in virtual environments. Using an option-action framework, asynchronous self-monitoring, and a prioritized memory system, Lyfe Agents operate at 10–100x lower computational cost than existing alternatives while maintaining sophisticated social behaviors like collaborative problem-solving.

Enhancing Understanding in Generative Agents through Active Inquiring
J. Ge, K. Zhao, M. Ernesto Cortes, Jovana Kondic, S. Luo, M. Naim, A. Ahn, G. R. Yang
NeurIPS Workshop on Intrinsically-Motivated and Open-Ended Learning, 2024
paper

We explore equipping LLM-powered agents with human-like curiosity: unlike humans, LLMs typically accept information passively without an inherent desire to question or doubt. We introduce an experimental framework where generative agents navigate strange and unfamiliar situations, and their understanding is assessed through interview questions, showing that active inquiring meaningfully improves agent comprehension.

Empowering Biomedical Discovery with AI Agents
S. Gao, A. Fang, Y. Huang, V. Giunchiglia, A. Noori, J. R. Schwarz, Y. Ektefaie, Jovana Kondic, M. Zitnik
Cell, 2024
paper

We envision collaborative AI agents that integrate LLMs, generative models, and biomedical tools to empower scientific research — from virtual cell simulation and phenotype control to the design of new therapies. Rather than replacing humans, these agents combine human creativity with AI's ability to navigate vast hypothesis spaces, plan discovery workflows, and perform self-assessment to identify knowledge gaps.

Bayesian Inverse Motion Planning for Online Goal Inference in Continuous Domains
T. Zhi-Xuan, Jovana Kondic, S. Slocum, J. B. Tenenbaum, V. K. Mansinghka, D. Hadfield-Menell
ICRA Workshop on Cognitive Modeling in Robot Learning for Adaptive Human-Robot Interactions, 2023
paper

We perform online goal inference and trajectory prediction in continuous domains by modeling agents as approximately Boltzmann-rational motion planners that produce low-cost trajectories while avoiding obstacles. Using a sequential Monte Carlo algorithm, we approximate the full posterior distribution over goals and future trajectories from partial, noisy observations in real time.

On the Critical Role of Conventions in Adaptive Human-AI Collaboration
A. Shih, A. Sawhney, Jovana Kondic, S. Ermon, D. Sadigh
ICLR, 2021
arXiv

We show that separating task-specific rules from partner-specific conventions is critical for adaptive human-AI collaboration. Our representation-learning framework disentangles these two types of knowledge, enabling zero-shot coordination on new tasks with familiar partners and rapid adaptation to new teammates, with up to 50% performance gains in coordination games including Hanabi.

Validation and Comparison of Instrumented Mouthguards for Measuring Head Kinematics and Assessing Brain Deformation in Football Impacts
Y. Liu, A. G. Domel, S. A. Yousefsani, Jovana Kondic, G. Grant, M. Zeineh, D. B. Camarillo
Annals of Biomedical Engineering, 2020
paper / arXiv

We validate and compare five instrumented mouthguards against a Hybrid III headform using simulated football impacts, evaluating their accuracy in measuring head kinematics and predicting brain deformation. All tested devices achieved mean relative errors below 13% for peak angular acceleration and below 8% for peak angular velocity, though accuracy varies with impact location.
